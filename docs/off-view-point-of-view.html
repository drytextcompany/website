From our meeting

What we heard.

we were listening.

Off-View is the affordable line, ₹1,000 to ₹1,500, built to stand against the big buy-one-get-one offers.
It should feel playful, young and a little cool. Wolf Eyes stays the serious one.
The products are made and already out there. Production is strong and stays exactly as it is.
One page for both brands, because paying two agencies for two brands is money you’d rather not spend.

Our read

What we see.

This isn’t a launch. It’s a discovery problem.

The glasses exist, the price is right, the quality is handled. What’s missing is anybody knowing the name. That changes the job: the first months are about being found and being remembered, not about a brand book nobody reads.

good product. quiet room.

The Wolf Eyes page is a trade counter, not a shop.

By your own read, most of those 3,500 followers are retailers and distributors: the people who stock you, not the twenty-two-year-old buying one pair. Posting Off-View there reaches the wrong room, and it asks two very different audiences to share one feed.

3,500 of the wrong people.

There’s no door to walk through.

The website is Wolf Eyes only. Off-View isn’t on it. So even when someone hears the name, there’s nowhere to land, nothing to read and nothing to buy. A page of its own on the existing site would fix that without touching anything else.

Voice, three ways

How Off-View could sound.

Three directions, each with the thought behind it and three lines written in that voice. Pick one and we build it out properly.

pick one. ignore two.

One. Glasses, not eyewear

Off-View treats specs as an outfit decision, not a medical purchase. Light, funny, never trying too hard. The enemy isn’t another brand, it’s the idea that buying glasses has to be boring.

“₹1,200. Less than the haircut you regret.”

“Specs for people who lose specs.”

“Looks expensive. Isn’t. That’s the whole pitch.”

Two. The second pair

Answers buy-one-get-one without playing the discount game. One pair is a habit, two is a wardrobe. It makes owning more pairs normal, which is exactly what sells an affordable line again and again.

“One pair is a personality. Two is a wardrobe.”

“Monday glasses. Friday glasses. Same face.”

“Your backup pair just got promoted.”

Three. Wolf Eyes, off duty

Borrows the parent’s credibility on purpose: the people who make serious eyewear, with the rules off. It’s the direction that makes a shared page make the most sense, and the one that needs the lightest touch to stay charming.

“Same factory. Fewer rules.”

“The serious eyewear people, after hours.”

“Wolf Eyes raised us. We don’t listen.”

this one needs the least new ground.

Worth settling first

Whose page does this live on?

You said one page, to keep the cost of two brands down. That’s fair, and it’s a money question, so here’s the money answer first: we charge for the work, not per brand. One team, one monthly price, whether it runs on one page or two.

one team. one price. two pages.

Option one · everything on the Wolf Eyes page

What works3,500 followers from day one, no new audience to build, and retailers who stock you see the new line immediately.

What it costsThose followers are trade. A playful post for a 22-year-old lands in a feed built for distributors, and the serious brand starts sounding unsure of itself. You also can’t tell which brand is working, because it’s all one number.

Option two · Off-View gets its own page, we run both

What worksThe right voice reaches the right room, Wolf Eyes keeps its trade face, and you can finally see what Off-View does on its own. Bolder content becomes possible, because it isn’t sitting next to a B2B post.

What it costsIt starts at zero followers and needs steady posting for the first couple of months. It does not cost more: the price is for the work we do each month, not the number of logos.

What we’d do

Open the Off-View page and give it sixty days, with Wolf Eyes pointing at it: a pinned post, a link in the bio, and the launch pieces shared across. If it hasn’t moved by then, we fold Off-View back into the main page as a named series and lose nothing but the handle. The downside is small, the upside is a brand with its own audience that you own.

Two ideas we’d run first

Where we’d start.

headlines now. detail once we’re in.

Drops that don’t touch the factory.

You liked the limited edition idea but not the production risk. So we drop everything except the frame: a festival case, a pouch, a cloth, a sticker set, an artist on the packaging. Same glasses you already hold, new story every few weeks, nothing new to manufacture.

Two pairs, not two for one.

The big players compete on buy-one-get-one, which trains people to wait for a sale. Off-View competes on owning more than one: a pair for work, a pair for the weekend, outfits and faces rather than discounts. It sells the second pair without cutting the price of the first.
