Faro Ahmedabad

Ahmedabad’s famous market streets, rebuilt in 3D. You make an avatar, land on the street and walk it — into the real shops, under their real names, standing where they actually stand. Shopkeepers pay to be on it properly: the interior, the catalogue, the offers, and a WhatsApp button that counts what it sends them.

Every shopkeeper on that street has been sold online marketing before and watched nothing happen. So Faro is built to prove itself: tracked buttons, and a code the customer shows at the counter, so the walk-ins are something the shopkeeper sees with his own eyes rather than something we claim in a report.

Gurukul Street, Memnagar · prototype, twelve shops · Law Garden and Manek Chowk next

faro means lighthouse. it guides people to the right place, which is the whole job.

Nom Nom

QR ordering for cafes. A guest scans the code on the table, reads the cafe’s own menu and orders from it. They still pay at the counter, because nobody asked for another payment screen. Staff see every order on one screen at the desk.

Three menus are built as working concepts: a chai house whose menu follows the time of day, a roastery you tune with your thumb, and a book cafe laid out like a library card catalogue. Looking for the first real cafe to put one on a table.

In design · three concept menus · first cafe wanted

built because every QR menu in this city is a PDF that refuses to zoom.

More when they’re ready.
