A round of revisions is whatever nobody defined.

Almost every quote for writing promises a number of revisions. Almost none of them say what one revision is, and that is where the arguing starts.

Granth Hirapara17 October 20266 minute read

the comma you changed on whatsapp at 11pm was, technically, a round.

The line usually sits near the bottom of the quote: “Includes two rounds of revisions.” The founder reads it, nods, and signs. Three weeks later the writer says the rounds are used up, and the founder is fairly sure they asked for one comma and a phone number. Both are telling the truth. They were counting different things.

So before the number, settle the definition. The number is the easy part.

Three kinds of change

Most disagreements come from treating every change as the same size. They aren't. There are roughly three:

  • A fix. A wrong figure, a misspelt name, a phone number from 2019. Small, factual, and done in minutes.
  • A revision. A set of notes on a draft that still answers the brief. Tighten this paragraph, this line sounds like a boast, move pricing higher up. The writer reworks it and sends a new version.
  • A rewrite. The direction has changed. A new audience, a new offer, or a realisation that the page belongs to the clinic, with the doctor second. The old draft is set aside and a new one begins.

A quote that says “two revisions” is almost always talking about the middle one. Write that down, and half the future argument is gone.

What counts as one round

A workable definition: one set of notes, from one person, in one document, answered by one new draft. The round opens when the notes are sent and closes when the new draft comes back.

What breaks it is the drip. A comment on Monday morning, two more on WhatsApp after lunch, a voice note on Tuesday from the partner who has just seen it. Is that one round or four? Nobody knows, so both sides keep score privately, and both scores are wrong. The fix is on the client's side and costs nothing: collect everything, then send it once. We wrote about how to make those notes useful in how to give feedback on a draft.

Some changes shouldn't count at all. If the writer got a fact wrong, putting it right is their job, and charging a round for it would be cheeky. The same goes for a typo, or a sentence that contradicts the brief you both agreed on.

Why the number is usually two or three

With a clear brief and notes that point at sentences, the second draft usually lands. A third round is insurance, for the reader who joins late or the fact that arrives on the last day. Past that, extra rounds rarely make the writing better. They make it different, and then different again.

Be careful with “unlimited revisions” offered with no conditions. Someone pays for round nine, usually in attention, or in how fast round ten comes back. Our own terms put a condition on it: no cap on revisions as long as there is a real reason, and each one back the same day or the next. Full rewrites are counted separately. Three are free, each takes three to four days, and a fourth costs half the original price. It is all on the services page, in large type, so nobody has to remember it.

When round four arrives

Round four is rarely a writing problem. It is usually a brief nobody wrote down, being written one draft at a time.

You can tell from the notes. They start to undo the notes from the round before. A new person has read it and wants a different page. The offer has moved since the brief. None of that can be fixed by editing harder.

The useful move is to stop drafting and have one short call. Agree what changed, write it into the brief, and call the next version what it is: a rewrite. That is fairer to both sides than pretending it is round five of the same job.

What to agree before the first draft

Five questions, answered in writing, before anyone starts:

  1. How many rounds are included?
  2. What is one round? (One set of notes, one new draft.)
  3. Who sends the notes? One name, so the writer isn't refereeing your office.
  4. How fast does each round come back?
  5. What does a full rewrite cost once the free ones run out?

A writer who answers all five in a line each is one you can work with. A writer who goes vague on the fifth is planning to discuss it later, and later is when it costs more. These sit well beside the questions in what content writing costs.

If there is a quote on your desk this week, send the writer one message: “What counts as one round?” The answer will tell you more about the next month than the price did.
