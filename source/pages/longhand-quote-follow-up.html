A follow-up should give them something to reply to.

The quote went out ten days ago and nothing has come back. “Just following up” tells the client you are waiting, which they knew. Here is what to send instead.

Granth Hirapara11 November 20266 minute read

blue ticks, no reply. the two most expensive marks in indian business.

An interior designer in Bodakdev sends a quote for a three-bedroom flat. The client said on the site visit that they wanted to start before the new year. The quote is read the same evening, and then nothing. A week later the designer writes “Hi, just following up on the quote. Please let me know.” That is read too.

Silence after a quote is rarely a no. Usually the client is comparing two other quotes, waiting for a spouse or a partner to look at it, or has a question they feel awkward asking. The follow-up's job is to make replying easy. Most follow-ups make it harder, because the only reply they invite is a decision the client has not made yet.

Add one thing the first message did not have

A follow-up with nothing new in it is a reminder, and a reminder asks the reader to feel slightly guilty. Give them a fact, an option or a question instead. Any one of these is enough:

  • A smaller first step. “If the full flat is a lot to commit to at once, we could start with the kitchen and the master bedroom, which comes to ₹4,20,000 of the total.”
  • A date that matters. “To finish before the new year, the carpenters would need to start by 20 November.”
  • The answer to a question they did not ask. “A few clients ask whether the quote includes hardware. It does, down to the hinges.”
  • A plain question. “Is the timeline still the same on your side?”

The last one is the most useful and the least used. It can be answered in four words, and the answer tells you more than a polite “will revert” ever does.

Write it for the person reading on a phone

Most follow-ups in India go on WhatsApp, and they are read between other things. Three lines is the right length. The first line says which quote, because the client may have three: “About the quote for the Bodakdev flat.” The second carries the new thing. The third asks one question.

If you are sending it by email, the same rules apply to the subject line. “Following up” is the subject of every unread mail in their inbox. “Kitchen first, if that helps the budget” gives them a reason to open it. Email subject lines goes into this further.

“Just following up” asks the client to make a decision. A good follow-up asks them a question they can already answer.

Leave out the words that sound like waiting

  • “Just”. It apologises for the message before the message has started.
  • “As per my last message”. It reads as a reprimand, however mildly you meant it.
  • “Any update?” The update is that they have not decided. Asking for it makes them say so.
  • An unasked discount. Dropping the price in a follow-up tells the client the first number was padded. If price is the problem, let them say it, then talk about scope.
  • Pressure you cannot back up. “This rate is valid only till Friday” works once. If it is still valid next Friday, they will not believe the next deadline either.

How many times, and how far apart

This part is opinion. Send the first follow-up about a week after the quote, which is long enough for a family or a partner to have looked at it. Send a second one a week or two after that, with a different new thing in it. Then send a last one that closes the loop politely.

The last one is worth writing well, because it often gets the reply the others did not. “I'll assume the timing isn't right for now and stop following up. If anything changes, this number reaches me directly.” It takes the pressure off, and people who were embarrassed to say no will often say so now, or say yes.

Between the second and third messages, stop. Four follow-ups to someone who has read every one is no longer a follow-up. It is a reason for them to block the number.

Make the first message do some of the work

The easiest follow-up is the one the quote has already set up. When you send a quote, end it with a date: “I'll call you on Thursday to answer any questions.” Then Thursday's call is a promise kept, and nobody feels chased. A quote that ends with “Awaiting your positive response” leaves you nothing to follow up with except the waiting.

We hold our own cold mails to the same rule. The note on follow-ups in 27 cold mails we sent says it in a line: follow up once, with something new.

Write yours now

Find the oldest quote you sent that has not had a reply. Write three lines: which quote it is, one thing the client did not have before, and one question they can answer without deciding anything. Read it as the client would, on a phone, between two other chats. If the only possible reply is yes or no, change the question. If you would like the quote itself to need fewer follow-ups, that is the kind of thing we write.
