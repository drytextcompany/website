# The loud eight — visiting cards built to be kept

Same kit as everything else: Paper `#FBFBF9`, Ink `#16161A`, Red `#D2232A`, Newsreader, Kalam,
JetBrains Mono, the caret. Nothing new was invented; the kit is just used without flinching.

Print-ready, exactly like the nine in the folder above: **page 1 is the front, page 2 is the back**,
the page *is* the card plus 3 mm of bleed, no margin and no crop marks. Trim 3 mm in on all sides.
Fonts embedded.

| File | The hook |
|---|---|
| `bold-1-the-typo.pdf` | The name is printed wrong — "The Dry Txt Co." — with the caret putting the *e* back. Under it: "One letter short. You spotted it in under a second. That is exactly what we get paid for." People check it twice, then keep it. |
| `bold-2-the-edit.pdf` | A line of corporate sludge struck through in red, the real sentence written above it in pen. The card demonstrates the service instead of describing it. |
| `bold-3-the-rejection.pdf` | An editor's rejection slip addressed to the brand: "Thank you for sending us your copy. Unfortunately it is not a fit for your readers at this time. We would, however, be glad to fix it." Ends in pen: *not a rejection. a rescue.* |
| `bold-4-the-blank-line.pdf` | Ruled lines and one instruction: "Write the sentence you hate:". The back has a QR that opens WhatsApp so they can send it. A card people write on never gets thrown away. |
| `bold-5-eight-seconds.pdf` | "Eight seconds." — what a homepage gets before somebody decides. "This card took four." |
| `bold-6-redacted.pdf` | "We'd tell you what we charge, but ███." Then, quietly: priced per project, once we understand it. |
| `bold-7-ten-lines.pdf` | Ten circles, the first one filled. One line on the house, the other nine invoiced, happily. A loyalty card for copywriting. |
| `bold-8-second-side.pdf` | Front: "We write copy so good—". Back: "—it needed a second side." The whole joke needs both hands. |

`previews/` has a 300 dpi picture of all sixteen sides for approvals. Not for print.

## If you only print one

`bold-1-the-typo` gets the strongest reaction, and it proves the service in one glance.
`bold-4-the-blank-line` is the one that starts conversations, because it asks for something.
`bold-8-second-side` is the one people show to somebody else.

## Careful with

**The typo card.** A few people will think it's a mistake before they see the caret. That risk is the
whole point, but don't hand it to somebody who will never turn it over: pair it with a plain card
from the folder above for banks, government offices and first meetings.
