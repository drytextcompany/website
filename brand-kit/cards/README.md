# Visiting cards — The Dry Text Co.

Nine finalised cards, print-ready. Everything here is built from the brand kit in `brand-kit/assets`:
same paper, ink and red, same three typefaces, the real wordmarks.

## What to send a printer

Send the PDF from `print-ready/` for the card you are printing. Each file has two pages:
**page 1 is the front, page 2 is the back.** Fonts are embedded, so nothing needs installing.

| `h4-five-things-horizontal.pdf` | The five things, plain list |

Do not send those to print.

## Specification

- **Trim:** 88.9 × 50.8 mm horizontal, 50.8 × 88.9 mm vertical (3.5 × 2 in).
- **Bleed:** 3 mm on all four edges, already in the files.
- **Crop marks:** on the trim lines, outside the bleed.
- **Safe area:** nothing important sits within 5 mm of the trim.
- **Colours:** Paper `#FBFBF9`, Ink `#16161A`, Red `#D2232A`.
  Ask for the red to be matched to the hex. Pantone 186 C is the nearest standard red;
  get a draw-down before the run, because a lazy conversion turns this red pink.
- **Stock:** 300–350 gsm uncoated, natural white. No gloss lamination: the whole brand is paper.
  If there is budget, letterpress the caret so it can be felt, or paint the edges red.
- **Ink coverage:** `h8`, `h9`, `h9b` and `v8` are solid-colour cards. Ask for them on an uncoated
  stock that takes heavy coverage without cracking at the fold, and expect a slightly darker result
  than the screen.

## Two things worth knowing

1. **The red cards break our own rule.** The brand kit says red is a pen, not a fill. `h9` and `h9b`
   are a deliberate exception for the founder's card, not a new habit for the whole team.
2. **If your printer works in 90 × 55 mm**, say so and the layouts get re-set: nothing is locked to
   the millimetre, but the files here are not the right size for that press.
